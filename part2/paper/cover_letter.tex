Dear Professor Korotkova,

I am submitting the manuscript ``Media-in-the-loop holography: designing
photopolymer exposures through a differentiable recording model'' for
consideration as a Research Article in \textit{JOSA A}.

Camera-in-the-loop correction has substantially improved computer-generated
holography on rewritable modulators, but it cannot be used with write-once volume
photopolymers, where the reconstruction error can be measured only after it is
permanent. The manuscript studies, in simulation, the alternative of designing
the recording exposure through a differentiable model of the recording itself:
the non-local polymerization-driven diffusion (NPDD) model introduced in this
journal by Sheridan and Lawrence [JOSA A \textbf{17}, 1108 (2000)], coupled to
split-step beam-propagation readout.

On a benchmark of \MOneNCells{} spatial-frequency and contrast-budget cells,
designing through the model improves on the same optimizer with a linear,
media-blind recording assumption in \MOneNPositive{} cells (by up to
\MaxGainEightX\,dB) and reduces the fraction of cells more than
\WastedMediaThreshDB\,dB short of an oracle reference from
\WastedMediaBSGDFrac\% to \WastedMediaMILFrac\%. A factorial ablation with a slope-matched linear
control shows that the gain requires a nonlinear dose response but no single
saturating mechanism. The study also reports where the approach fails: the gain
decays toward zero at high spatial frequency, largely vanishes for fringes
slanted by $10^\circ$ or more, and reverses if the model underestimates the
medium's index budget several-fold. The recording model reproduces published
Bayfol HX dose-response data, including a parameter-free prediction between a
measured and a modelled series.

The work sits within \textit{JOSA A}'s core topics of holography, diffraction
gratings and computational imaging, and should interest readers working on
holographic recording media, computer-generated holography and physics-based
inverse design. The claims are scoped to what the simulations support: every
number in the manuscript is generated by script from released per-job results,
and the code and data are public (Data availability).

\textbf{Declarations.} The manuscript is original, has not been published, and
is not under consideration by another journal. A companion study (Part~1, on
phase-only CGH with idealized modulators) is available as a preprint on Optica

results.
