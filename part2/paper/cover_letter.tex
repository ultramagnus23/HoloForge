Professor Peter E. Andersen, Editor-in-Chief, \textit{Applied Optics}

Dear Professor Andersen,

I am submitting ``Media-in-the-loop holography: designing photopolymer
exposures through a differentiable recording model'' for consideration as a
Research Article.

A laboratory recording a volume hologram in a photopolymer must choose the
exposure before the medium distorts the target, and can measure the error only
once it is permanent; camera-in-the-loop correction, which solves the
equivalent problem on a spatial light modulator, is unavailable. The manuscript
asks what is gained by designing the exposure through a model of the recording
instead, and answers three practical questions: when it is worth doing, when it
stops working, and what has to be measured first. The gain over a media-blind
design is positive in \MOneNPositive{} of \MOneNCells{} grid cells, up to
\MaxGainEightX\,dB, and removes every exposure falling more than
\WastedMediaThreshDB\,dB short of an oracle reference, which is the benefit
that matters when each attempt consumes a film. Section~6 is a step-by-step
calibration protocol with the minimum measurement set and the conditions for
not using the method; Section~4.6 states the geometric boundary and identifies
its cause. The work fits \textit{Applied Optics} in both of the ways the
journal asks for: it gives design guidelines, and it explains the limits of
implementing theory.

\textbf{Previous submission, disclosed in full.} An earlier version was
submitted to \textit{JOSA A} as manuscript ID 620231 and declined on 15 October
2026 by the Editor-in-Chief at the editorial stage, on the grounds that the
research area lies outside that journal's direct scope and that \textit{JOSA A}
restricts papers relying on computer-simulated results. It was not sent for
peer review, no reviews were returned, and no scientific objection to the work
was raised. I can supply the decision letter.

\textbf{What has changed since.} The manuscript now quantifies which recording
parameters the gain depends on, by two independent routes that agree, and finds
the medium's index budget dominating the other three by more than a factor of
three, with a several-fold underestimate reversing the benefit. It also
explains the method's dependence on fringe slant rather than merely reporting
it. The gain rises to a peak and then collapses, and both features follow from
one closed form: a fringe tilt of $\arcsin(K\lambda/4\pi n_0)$, the Bragg angle
itself, brings a normally illuminated grating onto the Bragg condition. That
predicts the peak angle at each tested spatial frequency with nothing fitted,
and the measurements agree. Supporting this, the split-step beam propagation
used inside the design loop is now compared with rigorous coupled-wave analysis
directly, across the full slant range, rather than only through the
closed-form reference as before; the two agree to within 0.04 in first-order
fraction everywhere. The main text is self-contained; Supplement~1 keeps only
full derivations and extra sweeps.

The limitations are stated plainly. The study is in simulation; no hologram has
been recorded. The model is compared with published dose-response data for one
commercial photopolymer at one spatial frequency, and the manuscript says why
that is not enough to establish transfer across spatial frequency or across
media, making that measurement the first item of the protocol it recommends to
others. Every number is generated by script from released per-job result files,
and the code and raw results are public.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and runs to
\param{9} pages in the two-column journal layout, with five figures, three
tables and a supplemental document. Part~1, a companion study on phase-only
computer-generated holography with idealized modulators, is a cited Optica Open
preprint and does not overlap with these results. I declare no conflicts of
interest and received no external funding; use of a generative AI assistant is
disclosed in the Acknowledgment, as Optica policy requires. Suggested
reviewers are entered in Prism.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
