\section*{S1: Small-signal compensation-cliff derivation, and the mechanism behind R1}
\label{sec:supp-s1}

\subsection*{Linearizing the recording equations}
This section derives the linearized recording transfer function $H(K)$
(the main text's Section 3.3 states $H$ as given; here it is derived),
defines $K_c(B_c)$ precisely as it is actually computed in the
pipeline, and verifies that the derivation reproduces the reported
values \KcPredTwoX/\KcPredFourX/\KcPredEightX\,rad/\textmu m at
$B_c \in \{2,4,8\}$ to three decimal places -- not approximately,
exactly, once the correct definition of $K_c$ is used.

Consider a spatially uniform mean exposure $I_{\text{mean}}$ with a small
sinusoidal perturbation, $I(x) = I_{\text{mean}}\big(1 + m\cos(Kx)\big)$,
$m \ll 1$. Two simplifications make the linearization closed-form and are
stated explicitly rather than left implicit: (i) dye depletion is
neglected, $d(x,t) \approx 1$, valid in the small-signal onset of
exposure this prediction targets, before bleaching has run appreciably;
(ii) the diffusivity is held at its bare value $D_0$ rather than the
accumulated-polymer-dependent $D_{\text{eff}}(N) = D_0
\exp(-\alpha_D N)$ -- i.e.\ the derivation linearizes around the
\emph{start} of the recording trajectory, not around its $N$-dependent,
time-varying diffusivity. Under these two simplifications the zeroth-order
(spatially uniform) fields obey $\dot{u}_0 = -F_0 u_0$,
$\dot{N}_0 = F_0 u_0$ with $F_0 = \kappa\, I_{\text{mean}}^\gamma$,
giving $u_0(t) = e^{-F_0 t}$. The first-order Fourier component at $K$ is
driven by this decaying mean field; adiabatically eliminating its fast
relaxation relative to the mean trajectory (quasi-steady balance,
$\dot{\widetilde{u}} \approx 0$) gives the fraction of the driving
perturbation that survives non-local smearing and deposits into $N$
before diffusing away:
\[
H(K) = \frac{\widehat{G_\sigma}(K)}{1 + D_0 K^2 / F_0}
     = \frac{\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}K^2\sigma^2)}{1 + D_0 K^2 / F_0},
\]
exactly the closed form implemented in
\texttt{NPDDRecorder.small\_signal\_mtf}. $H(K)$ is the fraction of a
small-signal exposure modulation at spatial frequency $K$ that survives
into recorded contrast: the Gaussian numerator is non-local smearing's
low-pass response, and the $1 + D_0 K^2/F_0$ denominator is diffusion's
own high-$K$ rolloff relative to the local reaction rate $F_0$.
Compensating a medium with response $H(K) < 1$ at frequency $K$ requires
pre-boosting the exposure there by $1/H(K)$; if $1/H(K)$ exceeds the
contrast budget $B_c$ actually available, that frequency cannot be fully
compensated, motivating a compensation-cliff hypothesis $K_c(B_c)$: the
frequency beyond which $1/H(K) > B_c$.

\subsection*{What $K_c(B_c)$ actually means in this pipeline}
A naive reading -- solve $1/H(K) = B_c$ for the \emph{nominal} budget
$B_c$ -- does \emph{not} reproduce the values reported in the main
text: at the default medium, this gives $K_c(2) = 4.22$,
$K_c(4) = 6.96$, $K_c(8) = 9.86\,\mathrm{rad}/\mu\mathrm{m}$ -- the third
value is $33\%$ off the reported \KcPredEightX. The pipeline's actual
definition (\texttt{analysis.aggregate.\_self\_consistent\_Kc}) compares
$1/H(K)$ not against the nominal budget but against the contrast
\emph{actually realized by the optimizer at that same measured} $K$,
$C(K)$ -- the smallest measured $K$ where $1/H(K)$ first exceeds $C(K)$,
linearly interpolated between grid points. Recomputing it from the logged
per-$K$ realized contrast reproduces $K_c = 4.254$, $6.963$,
$7.387\,\mathrm{rad}/\mu\mathrm{m}$ at $B_c \in \{2,4,8\}$ -- matching
\KcPredTwoX/\KcPredFourX/\KcPredEightX{} to three decimal places. The
reported values are correct as printed; they are the self-consistent
definition, not the naive one, and the naive form above is retained as
the wrong number a reader would get by the obvious misreading, not
smoothed over.

\subsection*{Why $K_c$ saturates between $B_c=4$ and $B_c=8$}
The reported $K_c$ scales $\times 1.638$ from $B_c=2\to4$ but only
$\times 1.061$ from $B_c=4\to8$ -- far more saturating than the Gaussian
kernel alone predicts (a pure-Gaussian-limited $H$ would give
$\times 1.41$, $\times 1.22$ under the same doubling). The cause is not a
property of $H(K)$'s functional shape: it is that the self-consistent
definition depends on \emph{realized}, not nominal, contrast, and the
optimizer's realized contrast tracks the nominal budget closely at
$B_c=2$ and $4$ (mean realized $2.03$, $3.98$ against nominal $2$, $4$)
but falls far short of nominal at $B_c=8$ (mean realized $6.03$, up to
$4.2$ below nominal at individual $K$ points). A budget the optimizer
cannot actually spend does not extend the compensable range the way the
same nominal number would under perfect realization.

\subsection*{Exposure and index-profile mechanism behind Figure R1}
\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{../code/figures/paper/R3_exposure_profiles.pdf}
\caption{Exposure $E(x)$ (left) and recorded index profile $\Delta n(x)$
(right) for media-blind SGD vs.\ media-in-the-loop, same $K$ points and
budget as the main text's R1 figure, seed \ROneSeedValue. Media-blind
SGD's exposure saturates $\Delta n(x)$ against $\Delta n_{\max}$ at
higher $K$; media-in-the-loop's does not.}
\end{figure}
Media-blind SGD's naive linear assumption drives $E(x)$ toward
near-binary swings at every tested $K$; at low $K$ this happens not to
conflict with the medium's response, but at high $K$ it pushes
$\Delta n(x)$ to clip against $\Delta n_{\max}$ almost everywhere,
wasting most of the available contrast budget on an index modulation the
medium cannot actually deliver. Media-in-the-loop's optimized $E(x)$ is
visibly less extreme, trading peak exposure for an index profile that
stays inside the medium's linear-to-saturating transition.

\section*{S2: Physics-component ablation (full conditions and figure)}
\label{sec:supp-s2}
The ablation removes one NPDD mechanism at a time ($\sigma\to0$ for
non-locality, $D_0\to0$ for diffusion, $k_{\text{bleach}}\to0$ for dye
depletion, $\Delta n_{\max}\to100\times$ for an unsaturating
linear-regime approximation) and re-measures paired gain at the three
sub-/near-/post-cliff $K$ shared with the compute-matched control,
budget $2\times$.
\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{../code/figures/paper/F7_physics_ablation.pdf}
\caption{Paired gain by ablation condition, at the three
sub-/near-/post-cliff $K$-points, budget $2\times$. Removing saturation
is qualitatively different from removing any other single mechanism.}
\end{figure}
Removing non-locality, diffusion, or dye depletion individually leaves
gain within \SOneNoDiffusionRatio--\SOneNoNonlocalityRatio$\times$ of the
\SOneBaselineGain~dB baseline (diffusion removal reduces gain the most
of the three, to \SOneNoDiffusionRatio$\times$ baseline; the other two
are within 5\%). Removing the saturation nonlinearity is qualitatively
different: gain rises to \SOneNoSaturationGain~dB,
\SOneNoSaturationRatio$\times$ the baseline.

\section*{S3: Parameter sensitivity (full grid and figure)}
\label{sec:supp-s3}
$D_0$, $\sigma$, and $\kappa$ are perturbed independently by
$\{-50,-10,0,10,50\}\%$ at four $K$ spanning the collapse region
(4.60--5.60\,rad/\textmu m), budget $2\times$.
\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth]{../code/figures/paper/F8_sensitivity_band.pdf}
\caption{Paired gain vs.\ parameter perturbation, one curve per NPDD
parameter ($D_0$, $\sigma$, $\kappa$), $K$-averaged over the
collapse-region grid, shaded 95\% CI. All three curves stay within a
narrow band across the full $\pm50\%$ perturbation range tested.}
\end{figure}
Paired gain is \STwoBaselineGain~dB at the unperturbed baseline and stays
within \STwoGainMin--\STwoGainMax~dB across every tested perturbation of
every parameter -- a narrow absolute range, in sharp contrast to the S2
ablations, where removing a mechanism entirely (not just perturbing its
value) changes gain by up to \SOneNoSaturationRatio$\times$. Both arms
are re-optimized on each perturbed medium here, so the perturbation is
common to both and cancels in the paired difference -- this establishes
sensitivity to the medium, not robustness to getting a parameter wrong
(main text Section on twin miscalibration tests that).

\section*{S4: Twin miscalibration (full per-parameter curves and figure)}
\label{sec:supp-s4}
$D_0$, $\sigma$, $\kappa$ and $\Delta n_{\max}$ are perturbed one at a
time by $\{\pm10, \pm25, \pm50\}\%$, at the same three
sub-/near-/post-cliff $K$ and budget $2\times$ used elsewhere, with the
exposure designed once at the nominal medium and evaluated, unchanged,
on the perturbed one.
\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth]{../code/figures/paper/F10_twin_mismatch.pdf}
\caption{Paired gain when the exposure is designed against the nominal
twin and recorded on a miscalibrated one, one curve per perturbed
parameter, $K$-averaged with 95\% CI over seeds. Shading marks the
$\pm50\%$ range the robustness claim is stated over. $\Delta n_{\max}$
is swept wider because our own literature fits disagree on it by
\BayfolDnMaxRatio$\times$; it is the only parameter whose curve reaches
zero, and only well outside $\pm50\%$.}
\end{figure}
Paired gain is \SThreeNominalGain~dB at nominal and stays between
\SThreeWorstWithinFifty\ and \SThreeBestWithinFifty~dB across every
tested perturbation of $D_0$, $\sigma$, $\kappa$. $\Delta n_{\max}$ is
the one parameter where the advantage is not simply safe in one
direction: the exposure is designed at the nominal $\Delta n_{\max}$ and
recorded on a medium whose true ceiling is perturbed relative to that
design, so a $\SThreeDnMaxMostNegativePct\%$ perturbation (true medium
holds \emph{less} capacity than the design assumed -- the design
over-estimates the true ceiling) leaves gain at
\SThreeDnMaxAtMostNegative~dB, essentially at the boundary of zero. A
$+\SThreeDnMaxFlipPct\%$ perturbation (true medium holds \emph{more}
than assumed -- the design under-estimates the true ceiling) is where
gain is worst, \SThreeDnMaxWorstGain~dB, having already gone negative
from \SThreeDnMaxAtHundred~dB at $+100\%$; this does not worsen
monotonically, though, since at the most extreme perturbation tested,
$+\SThreeDnMaxMostPositivePct\%$, gain partially recovers to
\SThreeDnMaxAtMostPositive~dB (still slightly negative). At the
magnitude of disagreement actually observed across independent
literature fits, $\Delta n_{\max}$ miscalibration is a real risk in both
directions.

\section*{S5: RCWA validity envelope}
\label{sec:supp-s5}
The original 3-case scalar-vs-vector cross-check (TE, unslanted,
near-Bragg incidence) agrees to \RCWAMaxDevThreeCase\ maximum absolute
diffraction-efficiency deviation. The \RCWAVGridNCases-case extension
(TE+TM, 3 incidence/slant geometries, multiple $\Delta n$ levels and $K$
values) finds \RCWAVGridMaxDev\ maximum deviation.
\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{../code/figures/paper/F3a_rcwa_validity_envelope.pdf}
\caption{Absolute deviation between Kogelnik coupled-wave theory and
RCWA first-order transmission efficiency, across the
\RCWAVGridNCases-case $K$/$\Delta n$/polarization grid, one series per
geometry. Unslanted geometries (Bragg and normal incidence) stay close
(max \RCWAUnslantedMaxDev, median \RCWANormalMedianDev for normal
incidence); the slanted $20^\circ$ geometry -- this paper's default --
diverges sharply at higher $K$, up to \RCWAVGridMaxDev\ absolute
deviation, concentrated at $K=12$\,rad/\textmu m: a 20-degree slant
detunes the Bragg condition at the nominal (unslanted-Bragg) incidence
angle, so RCWA correctly shows near-zero first-order diffraction while
the unslanted Kogelnik formula predicts near-peak efficiency. This is a
genuine finding about the unslanted formula's validity envelope, not a
numerical artifact, and is the reason BPM (not Kogelnik) is the model
used inside every optimization loop in this paper.}
\end{figure}
Readout caveat: the compensation-collapse result (main text) is a
\emph{recording}-side phenomenon; this grid characterizes
\emph{readout}-side scalar-vs-vector error, which moves absolute PSNRs
more than paired gains -- it scopes the readout engine's validity
envelope, it does not itself validate or invalidate the recording-side
result.

\section*{S7: Calibrating this twin for a new medium (full guidance)}
\label{sec:supp-s7}
What the parameters cost to obtain is uneven, and our own fitting
experience (main text, Twin Validation) separates them cleanly. Two are
identifiable from a single dose-response growth curve, which is the
measurement most sources already publish: the dose sensitivity $\kappa$
sets where the curve rises and the saturation ceiling $\Delta n_{\max}$
sets where it levels off, and fitting the pair jointly against one
$\Delta n_1$(dose) series was enough to reach NRMSE
\NRMSEBestInRegime--\NRMSEWorstInRegime. Two are not: the monomer
diffusivity $D_0$ and the non-locality length $\sigma$ are only weakly
constrained by a single-$K$ growth curve, because their signature is the
\emph{spatial-frequency dependence} of the response -- they need either a
multi-$K$ growth-curve family or an independent measurement, and
substituting $D_0$ for $\Delta n_{\max}$ as the second free parameter is
precisely the mistake that produced our initial poor fits. The
non-obvious lesson is about provenance rather than procedure:
$\Delta n_{\max}$ must be taken from the same dosage-response condition
being fitted. Published saturation ceilings are frequently reported from
separate screening experiments at different compositions or geometries,
and a value imported across that boundary looks authoritative while
being simply the wrong number -- it cost us a full round of fits before
the source's own text disclosed the mismatch. Finally, calibrate against
the shape and not only the endpoint: the rise-then-decline in
$\Delta n_1$(dose) is diagnostic, since it is produced by diffusive
smoothing of the recorded fringe rather than by the saturation ceiling,
and a fit that reproduces the peak height but not the decline has not
identified the transport parameters at all.

\section*{S8: Target-content and readout-noise robustness (full values)}
\label{sec:supp-s8}
At a single representative near-cliff $K$, three target kinds are
tested: the periodic bars used throughout, a sparse random-spots
target, and an uncorrelated random binary pattern. Paired gain:
\SFourGainBarsTwoX--\SFourGainBarsEightX~dB for bars,
\SFourGainSpotsTwoX--\SFourGainSpotsEightX~dB for spots (the largest of
the three, bootstrap CI within [\SFourBootCILoSpotsTwoX,
\SFourBootCIHiSpotsEightX]~dB), and
\SFourGainRandomBinaryTwoX--\SFourGainRandomBinaryEightX~dB for random
binary (the smallest, though still clearly positive), across the

\SFiveNoiseStdPct\% readout noise, at the same representative point.

\section*{S9: Non-local kernel correction note}
\label{sec:supp-s9}
An earlier implementation applied the non-local Gaussian kernel
$G_\sigma$ to the bare monomer field $u$ ($F \cdot (G_\sigma * u)$)
rather than to the production term ($G_\sigma * (F u)$, the form used
throughout this paper). The two are not equivalent: applying $G_\sigma$
to $u$ alone leaves $\sigma$ with no effect on the recorded fringe
contrast, since $u$ is consumed pointwise by $F$ regardless of where the
kernel later moves it -- a chain initiated at $x'$ must deposit polymer
at $x$, so the smearing kernel has to act on where polymer is actually
\emph{produced}. This was caught and corrected before any of the results
reported in this paper were generated.

\section*{Gradient-Pathway Ablation}
Checkpointed discrete-adjoint gradients match unrolled-autodiff gradients
to cosine similarity \AblationCheckpointCosSim\ on a realistic
reconstruction-loss probe; a neural surrogate reaches
\AblationSurrogateCosSim. See Fig.~F9a in the released code's figure set.

\section*{GPU Mesh-Density Convergence}
Reconstruction PSNR is mesh-independent to within \MeshPSNRSpread~dB
across a $4\times$ resolution range ($n_x=512/1024/2048$, fixed 51.2\,$\mu$m
physical window); wall-clock was essentially flat across the same range on
a single GPU (single seed) -- the optimizer is overhead-bound, not
compute-bound, at this problem size, which motivated the batched
implementation used for the full manifest pipeline. See Fig.~F9b in the
released code's figure set.

\section*{Wavelength-Detuning Readout Sweep}
A hologram recorded at the 405\,nm design wavelength reaches
\WavelengthDesignPSNR~dB at that wavelength; reading the same recorded
profile out at 400--450\,nm shows a \emph{non-monotonic} falloff away
from the design point, consistent with Kogelnik's oscillatory
$\sin^2(\nu)$ angular/wavelength detuning response rather than a smooth
rolloff (single seed, single GPU run). See Fig.~F9c in the released
code's figure set.
