Dear Editor,

I submit "Media-in-the-Loop Holography: Computer-Generated Holography
Through a Differentiable Model of Volume Photopolymer Recording" for
consideration as a Research Article in Applied Optics.

**What the paper does.** Camera-in-the-loop correction is unavailable for
write-once volume photopolymers, because the reconstruction error can be
measured only after it is permanent. The paper studies, in simulation, the
alternative of optimizing the recording exposure through a differentiable
model of the recording chemistry (non-local polymerization-driven diffusion)
coupled to split-step beam-propagation readout, and compares it with the same
optimizer run under a linear, media-blind recording assumption.

**What it finds.** On an unslanted one-dimensional grating benchmark
(10 spatial frequencies x 3 contrast budgets), the simulated gain is positive
in 29 of 30 cells and decays toward zero at high spatial frequency; the
small-signal "compensation cliff" I derived is not observed as a persistent
sign change. Measured against an oracle reference, the fraction of exposures
more than 2 dB short falls from 37% to 0%, while at a 1 dB threshold the two
methods are similar. A factorial ablation shows that the gain requires a
nonlinear dose response (a slope-matched linear control gives essentially
none at low spatial frequency) and that no single saturating mechanism is
necessary. The gain largely disappears for fringes slanted by 10 degrees or
more, which is why the headline results are restricted to unslanted gratings.

**On scope and evidence.** This is a simulation study. The recording model is
checked against published Bayfol HX dose-response data, including a held-out
prediction between two series, but not against a new physical experiment, and
cross-spatial-frequency validation awaits multi-K data I could not access.
Every number in the manuscript is generated by script from released per-job
results, each claim is labelled as literature-reported, a stated parameter,
derived, or simulated, and a supplementary section lists the statements from
an earlier draft that the regenerated data no longer support.

I believe the work will interest readers working on holographic recording
media, computer-generated holography and differentiable physical modelling.

All source code, experiment manifests and raw results are publicly available
under the Apache 2.0 license (see Data availability), with reproduction
instructions in the repository.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
