Camera-in-the-loop correction cannot be applied to write-once volume photopolymers, whose reconstruction error is measured only once it is permanent. We study, in simulation, optimizing the recording exposure through a differentiable non-local photopolymerization-diffusion model with beam-propagation readout. On an unslanted grating benchmark of 30 spatial-frequency and contrast-budget cells, the gain over a media-blind optimizer is positive in 29 cells (at most 2.52 dB) and decays toward zero at high spatial frequency; the share of cells more than 2 dB below an oracle reference falls from 37% to 0%. A slope-matched linear-recording control shows that the gain requires a nonlinear dose response, and it largely vanishes for fringes slanted by 10° or more.
