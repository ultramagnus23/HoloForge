A write-once volume photopolymer must be exposed before the medium distorts the target, and the error is measurable only once it is permanent. We optimize the exposure through a differentiable recording model: non-local polymerization-driven diffusion read out by beam propagation. Over 30 spatial-frequency and contrast-budget cells the designed exposure beats a media-blind optimizer in 29, by up to 2.52 dB, and removes every exposure falling more than 2 dB short of an oracle reference. The gain needs a nonlinear dose response, depends most on the index budget, and vanishes once the fringes tilt. We give the calibration measurements required and when not to use the method.
